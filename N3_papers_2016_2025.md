# JLPT N3 papers, 2016–2025: master source guide

**This file does not contain full examination papers and does not fulfill the requested complete full-text corpus.** It provides verified source links, availability findings and gaps as a substitute. Annual full papers are not publicly released by the organizers, and the questions are copyrighted. No complete session package was authenticated or copied in this research. Checked 27 October 2026.

The [official JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html) explains that complete questions from each examination are not published. The [Japan Foundation Beijing statement](https://www.jpfbj.cn/jp/sys/?p=4340) further says that, apart from official workbooks, post-2010 examination questions are not publicly released and permission to use actual questions is not granted to individuals or organizations. These statements prevent treating an unofficial site's “official paper” label as evidence of an authorized release.

## Scope

“Last ten years” is interpreted here as the **last ten complete calendar years: 2016–2025**. That gives 20 scheduled July/December slots, of which 19 were administered. July 2020 was cancelled worldwide. December 2020 took place, although availability varied by location. [Cancellation announcement](https://www.jpf.go.jp/e/about/press/2020/001.html), [December 2020 test data](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/statistics/archive/202002.html).

A rolling October 2016–October 2026 window would instead exclude July 2016 and include July 2026. July 2026 is outside this guide's chosen scope; December 2026 is still in the future as of the research date. [Official 2026 dates](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html).

## Official material to use first

Open the [official practice-workbook index](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html). It provides the **2018 Volume 2** and **2012 workbook** sections. Each is a selection of questions used since the 2010 revision, approximately test-length; neither represents a named July or December examination.

For each edition, follow the **N3** row in these three areas:

1. **Test items:** vocabulary, grammar, reading and listening question PDFs.
2. **Listening audio files:** N3 Q1–Q5, covering the five listening task types.
3. **Other:** sample answer sheet, answers and listening script.

Keep each edition's questions, audio, answers and script together. These are the recommended official study materials; they cannot be substituted into a historical session and counted as that session's paper. The index also states the applicable reproduction conditions. [Official index](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html).

## Source distinctions that affect study use

- [JLPT Practice Test](https://www.jlptpracticetest.com/n3) calls its dated text sets independently republished and unaffiliated. Separate listening mocks do not supply the missing historical listening sections.
- [Kosei][kosei] has broad component labels but its file payloads were not inspected. It omits 2020; December 2023 is explicitly unofficial reference material.
- [HanoiLink][hanoi] lists both sessions of 2022–2024, but its 2025 row still says “updating.” The headline alone is not availability evidence.
- [PassJapanese](https://passjapanese.com/ja/jlpt/n3/full-exams) describes past-paper-format **mocks**, generally VIP access and no PDF downloads. Exclude them from historical occurrence counts.
- [OK JLPT](https://okjlpt.com/en/n3/papers) advertises historical sets, audio and answers, largely behind paid access. Only its public inventory was inspected.
- [De Thi Tieng Nhat](https://dethitiengnhat.com/en/jlpt/N3) now says it stopped publishing official examination questions and offers newly created mocks. Older recommendations to its former archive are stale.
- Answer-number tables, recall posts and protected explanation pages are not complete exam text. No paywall was bypassed or unofficial scan downloaded for this guide.

## Detailed audits and corpus status

See [2016–2020 source audit](research_2016_2020.md) and [2021–2025 source audit](research_2021_2025.md) for additional sources, language coverage, failed fetches and session-specific evidence.

The result is **19 administered-session discovery records, one cancelled slot, and zero authenticated full session packages**. There is therefore no defensible basis here for claiming every historical vocabulary item, every grammar structure or measured ten-year occurrence frequencies. Such analysis requires verified question text, options, passages and listening scripts, with a traceable definition of what counts as an occurrence. The official workbooks remain useful study material without making that historical-corpus claim.
