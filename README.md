# JLPT N3 research and study pack

This folder covers the last ten complete calendar years, **2016–2025**. It records all 20 scheduled July/December slots: 19 administered sessions and the worldwide-cancelled July 2020 slot.

Start with these four files:

1. [N3 papers, 2016–2025](N3_papers_2016_2025.md) — the 20-slot source guide, official materials, and session-by-session third-party discovery leads.
2. [N3 vocabulary](N3_vocabulary.md) — 300 curated study words with readings and concise English meanings.
3. [N3 grammar](N3_grammar.md) — 80 explained patterns with original examples and translations.
4. [Most frequent vocabulary](N3_most_used_vocabulary.md) — reproducible top-50 counts from the five readable official workbook components inspected here.

## What the research established

The JLPT organizers do not publish each post-2010 examination as a complete annual paper. The [official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html) says the exact questions from every examination are not published, and the [Japan Foundation release-policy notice](https://www.jpfbj.cn/jp/sys/?p=4340) says actual post-2010 questions are not publicly released apart from the official workbooks. The organizers also hold the test-question copyrights.

For that reason, this folder does not reproduce 19 complete copyrighted papers or claim that unofficial files are authenticated. The source guide records what each site visibly offers and distinguishes official workbooks, text-only republication claims, answer-only pages, recalled reconstructions, mocks, paywalled listings, and missing components.

The reliable official study baseline is the [JLPT practice-workbook index](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html). Its 2012 and 2018 editions each contain roughly one test's quantity of selected questions, plus answers, listening scripts, and audio. They are compiled workbooks and cannot be assigned to named July/December sessions.

## Suggested study order

Use an official workbook as a timed baseline. Review the 50 measured high-frequency words, then work through one vocabulary topic and five grammar entries per day. Return to the official questions after each review cycle. Treat third-party dated sets as unverified practice material unless their provenance and completeness can be established independently.
