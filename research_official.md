# JLPT N3: official-source research notes

Research date: 27 October 2026. Requested historical window: 2016–2025 inclusive. This document records source findings and access limitations; it is not a transcription of examinations.

## What can be established

- The requested window contains **20 calendar slots**, not 20 completed worldwide administrations: July 2020 was cancelled in Japan and overseas. The cancellation was announced on 19 May 2020. A study corpus must mark that slot **cancelled / not applicable**, not missing or zero occurrences. [Official cancellation notice](https://www.jlpt.jp/sp/e/topics/202005191589858711.html)
- Japan Foundation Beijing states that actual post-2010 examination questions have not been publicly released except through the official workbooks, and that other actual-question use is not authorized. Consequently, a third-party file labelled with a date does not establish an authorized, complete, accurate paper. [Japan Foundation release-policy notice](https://www.jpfbj.cn/jp/sys/?p=4340)
- The official FAQ confirms that individual examinations are not published as complete matching papers. It also explains that post-2010 vocabulary, kanji, and grammar specifications lists are not published. Therefore, an exhaustive official “N3 grammar list” or ten-year word-frequency table cannot be inferred from an ordinary study list. The FAQ currently cautions that tests may differ by country or region; a year and month alone need not identify a unique form. [Official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html)
- The **2012 Official Practice Workbook** and **2018 Official Practice Workbook Vol. 2** each select previously used questions from the revised test, approximately one examination's quantity per level. **2012 and 2018 are publication years, not proven test-administration dates.** Neither workbook supplies the complete 2016–2025 corpus. [Official workbook index](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html)

## N3 structure relevant to studying

The current official format lists vocabulary at **30 minutes**, grammar plus reading at **70 minutes**, and listening at **40 minutes**. Listening duration can vary slightly. N3 vocabulary has five item types; grammar has form selection, sentence assembly, and passage grammar; reading has short, medium, long, and information-search tasks; listening has five types, including immediate responses. [Official format](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html)

Scoring combines vocabulary and grammar into one 0–60 section, with separate 0–60 reading and listening sections. N3 requires at least **95/180 overall** and **19/60 in every section**. These are scaled scores, so a practice-test raw percentage is not an official score conversion. [Official scoring structure](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html), [FAQ on scaled scores](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html)

These observations do not establish how often a pattern occurs elsewhere, whether other options recur, or which administration originally supplied an item.

## Consequences for a ten-year frequency study

1. Keep the requested 20-slot manifest separate from the actual analyzed-document inventory.
2. Record workbook publication year separately from administration year/month; leave the latter unknown where it is not published.
3. Mark unavailable papers **unknown**, not zero. Exclude the cancelled slot from any administration denominator.
4. Give vocabulary and grammar counts only for a precisely identified, lawfully available, actually inspected corpus. Distinguish question stems, correct options, distractors, readings, and listening transcripts.
5. Count normalized lemmas separately from raw strings; document inflections, compounds, duplicate questions, and overlapping grammar patterns.
6. Label original study supplements as editorial teaching material. No ranking in such a supplement is evidence of examination recurrence.

This research does **not** establish an exhaustive, authenticated 2016–2025 N3 question corpus. The accompanying grammar file provides 80 original study entries with that limitation stated explicitly.
